## The Core Idea: Intelligence as Selective Forgetting

### Learning from Biology

Human intelligence works by **selective forgetting performed at biological speed**—we discard 99% of what our senses pick up, preserving just the 1% needed for action. This isn't a flaw—it's how intelligence works. Our minds construct **sparse representations** of reality that retain only what matters for survival, decision-making, and social interaction.

Cognitive Compression applies this natural wisdom to artificial systems by asking: *How can a system keep only the most important information while still making smart decisions?* This is an applied study of **Kolmogorov meaning**—finding the shortest program that reproduces experience without destroying significance.

This creates a system where information flows efficiently without compromising security or ethical standards.

Cognitive Compression works through four interconnected processes that transform raw data into useful knowledge. These **gestures** form a closed loop where entropy is expelled during compression and reintroduced during reconstruction:

### 2. Semantic Distillation: Dropping Dimensions That Don't Flip Decisions

The distillation process identifies and removes **latent dimensions whose absence does not flip downstream decisions**. The system evaluates which dimensions contribute to performance and maps full representations to minimal necessary information. This precision ensures compression preserves exactly what's needed for good decision-making while eliminating **ontological redundancy**.

*Governed by*: Mutual-information gradients that guide the distillation process.

### 3. Contextual Re-indexing: Reattaching Vectors to Situational Tags

Re-indexing adds contextual tags that make information retrievable based on role, time, and purpose. The system marks information with organizational relevance indicators and specifies validity periods. This advanced approach uses **multidimensional hashing** and **semantic pointers** to enable quick searching across multiple contexts, ensuring the right information reaches the right people at the right time.

### 4. Reconstructive Resonance: Re-hydrating via Holographic Completion

When information is needed, the system expands compressed knowledge into appropriate responses based on context. Unlike traditional decompression that aims for perfect reconstruction, **reconstructive resonance** is generative and context-sensitive—using compressed representations as seeds for responses tailored to current needs.

*Mechanisms*: Retrieval-augmented generation and associative memory networks guide this process.
